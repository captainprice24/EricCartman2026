## v01-05-c

Tardigrades, sometimes called water bears, are microscopic animals rarely longer than half a millimetre. More than 1,000 species have been described, living in habitats from deep-sea sediments to the damp moss on city walls. They are famous for surviving extreme cold, intense radiation and prolonged drying out.

Their main survival strategy is a state known as cryptobiosis. As its surroundings dry out, a tardigrade curls into a compact 'tun', loses almost all of its body water and reduces its metabolism to an almost undetectable level. In this state some individuals have revived after years, and in a 2007 experiment some even survived exposure to the vacuum of space. Researchers have also identified proteins that appear to protect tardigrade cells and DNA while they are dried out. Strictly speaking, however, tardigrades are not extremophiles: they merely endure extreme conditions until normal ones return, and active tardigrades are no more resistant than many other small invertebrates.
