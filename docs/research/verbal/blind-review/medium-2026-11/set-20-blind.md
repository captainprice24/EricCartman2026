## v20-01

For more than two decades, all Member States have moved their clocks forward on the last Sunday of March and back on the last Sunday of October, under a directive that harmonised the dates to protect the single market. The directive does not decide which time zone a country belongs to; that choice remains national, so the Union spans three standard time zones.

In 2018 a Commission consultation received about 4.6 million replies, 84 per cent of them in favour of ending the twice-yearly change, although around two thirds came from Germany and the respondents were self-selected. The Commission then proposed abolishing the change and letting each Member State choose permanent summer or winter time. Parliament approved the proposal in 2019, but the Council has not adopted a position: several governments fear a patchwork of time zones, precisely what the original directive sought to avoid.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. The directive requires every Member State to use the same time zone.
B. The Council adopted the proposal in 2019, after Parliament had amended it.
C. The proposal revives a risk that the harmonising directive was meant to prevent.
D. German respondents usually preferred permanent summer time.

## v20-02

In April 1815 Mount Tambora, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, produced the most powerful eruption in recorded history. Tens of thousands of people died, most of them not from the blast itself but from the famine and disease that followed the destruction of crops. The eruption also injected sulphur into the stratosphere, where tiny droplets reflected part of the incoming sunlight.

The effects reached far away, after a delay. In 1816, the 'year without a summer', frosts struck New England in June and persistent rain ruined harvests across much of Europe, where grain prices soared and food riots broke out. Historians caution against blaming every hardship on the volcano: Europe was still recovering from the Napoleonic Wars, and weak solar activity may have added to the cold. Climate scientists nonetheless treat Tambora as a natural experiment showing how one eruption can cool the Northern Hemisphere for a year or more.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. The eruption alone explains Europe's cold weather and food riots of 1816.
B. The ruined European harvests came in the year after Tambora's eruption.
C. Most of the victims on Sumbawa were killed directly by the blast.
D. Climate scientists generally recommend cooling the planet with particles.

## v20-03

A placebo is a treatment with no active ingredient, such as a sugar pill. Patients often improve simply from receiving care, a response shaped by expectation, the ritual of treatment and the natural ups and downs of illness. The effect is strongest for symptoms patients judge themselves, such as pain or nausea, and has little measurable influence on outcomes such as tumour size.

For a long time, researchers assumed that a placebo could work only if the patient believed it to be a real medicine, so prescribing one seemed to require deception, which most ethicists reject. Since around 2010, however, small trials of 'open-label' placebos, in which patients are told the pills are inactive, have found that some people with irritable bowel syndrome or chronic back pain still report more relief than those given nothing. Sceptics note that the trials could not be blinded and that the benefits were modest.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. Most ethicists would accept the routine use of open-label placebos.
B. The placebo response is generally strongest in patients who distrust their doctors.
C. Open-label placebos have brought relief to all patients with irritable bowel syndrome.
D. Open-label trials question the old view that placebos work only on deceived patients.

## v20-04

According to a much-cited atlas published in 2016, more than a third of humanity can no longer see the Milky Way from home, a share that rises to around 60 per cent in Europe. Biologists now treat light pollution as an environmental problem in its own right. Newly hatched sea turtles head for the brightest horizon, which near a lit promenade may be a road rather than the sea; migrating birds are drawn to illuminated buildings; and in humans, bright evening light delays the release of melatonin, a hormone involved in sleep.

Unlike many pollutants, light disappears the moment its source is switched off, so remedies can act quickly: shielding lamps, dimming them after midnight and choosing warmer colours all reduce the glow. The switch to LED street lighting has had mixed effects, cutting electricity use but encouraging some towns to install more lights.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. Light pollution can be reduced faster than many other forms of pollution.
B. Sea turtles usually hatch on coasts that have been developed for tourism.
C. LED street lighting has reduced the glow above most European towns.
D. Bright evening light can stop the body from producing melatonin at all.

## v20-05

In the early twentieth century, the archaeologist Arthur Evans unearthed thousands of clay tablets at Knossos on Crete, written in a script he called Linear B. Evans was convinced that the language was not Greek, and his authority discouraged others from testing that idea for decades. In 1952 the architect Michael Ventris, who had himself expected a different language, deciphered the script and showed that it recorded an early form of Greek, centuries older than the first Greek written in the alphabet.

The tablets are mostly records of rations, livestock and land, and they survived largely by accident: unbaked clay normally crumbles, but the fires that destroyed the palaces hardened them. An older Cretan script, Linear A, uses many similar signs but remains undeciphered, because reading it with Linear B sound values produces words that match no known language.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. The tablets record nothing other than rations, livestock and land.
B. Ventris set out to confirm Evans's view that Linear B recorded Greek.
C. Linear A probably records a language related to Greek that no one can read.
D. Without the palace fires, most tablets would probably have crumbled away.

## v20-06

The Hanseatic League, a network of merchant towns led by Lübeck, dominated trade in the Baltic and North Seas from the thirteenth to the fifteenth century. Unlike a state, it had no permanent army, no common treasury and no fixed list of members, and its assembly, the Diet, met irregularly and could not compel towns to attend. Its power rested instead on trading privileges in foreign offices such as those in London and Bergen; when a ruler threatened them, the towns could respond with a collective embargo.

From the late fifteenth century the League declined, as territorial states grew stronger, Dutch and English merchants competed for Baltic trade and Atlantic routes shifted commerce westwards. The last Diet met in 1669, although Lübeck, Hamburg and Bremen still called themselves Hanseatic cities long afterwards.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. By 1669 Hamburg and Bremen had stopped calling themselves Hanseatic cities.
B. Towns would boycott a hostile ruler, yet the Diet had no power to make them attend.
C. The League could fund its wars from a common treasury paid into by all its towns.
D. The League's decline was caused entirely by the new Atlantic trade routes.

## v20-07

The Rosetta Stone was found in 1799 by French soldiers near the town of Rashid, or Rosetta, in the Nile Delta. It carries a decree of 196 BC written three times: in hieroglyphs, in Demotic, the everyday Egyptian script, and in ancient Greek. After the French surrender in Egypt it passed to the British, and it has been in the British Museum since 1802.

Because scholars could read the Greek, the stone offered a key to the other scripts, but not at once. Thomas Young showed in the 1810s that some hieroglyphs stood for sounds, notably in royal names, and in 1822 Jean-François Champollion demonstrated that the script combined sound signs with signs for whole words, helped by his knowledge of Coptic. The stone's upper portion is missing, so the hieroglyphic text is the least complete of the three; copies of the decree found elsewhere have helped to fill the gaps.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. Champollion deciphered the hieroglyphs within months of the stone's discovery.
B. Thomas Young was probably the first to read the stone's Demotic text.
C. Not all of the decree's hieroglyphic text survives on the stone itself.
D. France has formally asked the British Museum to return the stone.

## v20-08

Sleep was once seen as a passive state, but research has overturned that view. During deep, slow-wave sleep the brain appears to replay activity recorded during the day, which is thought to help move new memories from the hippocampus to the cortex for longer storage. In typical experiments, volunteers learn word pairs or a sequence of finger movements and are tested after a night's sleep or an equal time awake; those who sleep usually perform better, and the advantage is often larger for material learned shortly before bed.

The findings are easily overstated. Studying late into the night at the expense of sleep may impair the retention of what has been learned. But claims that people can learn new languages by listening to recordings while asleep have little support, as the brain does not seem to encode complex new information during deep sleep.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. Studying late at night instead of sleeping may impair what a student retains.
B. Sleeping after learning guarantees a better result in a later test.
C. Deep sleep has been shown to move every new memory into the cortex.
D. People who sleep after practice may need fewer sessions to master a task.

## v20-09

Standard economic theory predicts that a minimum wage set above the market level reduces employment, because employers hire fewer workers whose output is worth less than the new wage. In 1994 the economists David Card and Alan Krueger challenged this prediction. They compared fast-food restaurants in New Jersey, which had raised its minimum wage in 1992, with similar restaurants in neighbouring Pennsylvania, where it had not changed, and found no evidence that employment fell in New Jersey.

Critics questioned the telephone survey behind the study, and a re-analysis with payroll records initially reported job losses, although later work with official data broadly confirmed the original result. Most later studies find small or no employment effects for moderate increases, but economists still disagree about large increases and about effects on hours and prices. Card shared the 2021 Nobel Prize in economics, partly for this work.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. Later research has settled the debate over large minimum wage increases.
B. Pennsylvania raised its minimum wage in 1992 by the same amount as New Jersey.
C. The New Jersey findings did not match what standard theory predicts.
D. Prices in New Jersey restaurants probably rose enough to offset the wage rise.

## v20-10

Peatlands are wetlands where waterlogged conditions slow the decay of dead plants, so that partly decomposed material builds up over thousands of years. Although they cover only about three per cent of the world's land, they store more carbon than all its forests combined. Many European peatlands were drained for farming, forestry or fuel, and once the water table falls, oxygen reaches the peat, which decomposes and releases carbon dioxide; in some Member States, drained peatlands account for a significant share of emissions from agricultural land.

Rewetting, which blocks drainage ditches to raise the water table, can stop most of these emissions, although it does not quickly restore the capacity to accumulate new carbon, and rewetted land may emit methane in the first years. Because rewetting usually ends conventional farming, schemes that pay farmers for wet farming are being tested in several countries.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. A rewetted peatland quickly regains its capacity to store new carbon.
B. Rewetting can cut most peat emissions yet may release methane at first.
C. Payment schemes for wet farming have probably ended most drainage in Europe.
D. Drained peatlands cause most farmland emissions in every Member State.
