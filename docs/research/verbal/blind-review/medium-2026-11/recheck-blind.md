# Blind review: reworded items

For each item, choose the one statement the text supports. Label every other option False (the text contradicts it), Not supported (the text does not say it) or Overstated (stronger than the text). Use only the text.

## v22-03

The human body hosts trillions of microorganisms, most of them bacteria in the gut. Textbooks long said they outnumbered human cells by ten to one, but a 2016 recalculation put the ratio closer to one to one. Gut bacteria break down dietary fibre that human enzymes cannot digest, and some produce vitamins such as vitamin K.

People with conditions such as obesity or type 2 diabetes often have a different mix of gut bacteria from healthy people. Such associations do not show which comes first: a disease, or the medicines and diet that accompany it, may change the microbiome rather than the other way round. Experiments in mice suggest a causal role, but evidence in humans is weaker. One treatment is well established: transplanting faecal bacteria from a healthy donor is highly effective against recurrent infections with Clostridioides difficile, which often flourishes after antibiotics.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. The 2016 recalculation also found more microbial species than expected.
B. Faecal transplants usually work against obesity as well as C. difficile.
C. Gut bacteria are needed to digest all of the foods that humans eat.
D. A different gut mix in sick people could be a result of their disease.

## v23-01

Since 1974 the European Commission has commissioned regular surveys of public opinion known as the Standard Eurobarometer. Each wave interviews about 1,000 people aged 15 and over in every Member State, although only about 500 are interviewed in the smallest countries, Cyprus, Luxembourg and Malta. Interviews were traditionally conducted face to face, but during the COVID-19 pandemic some were moved online. Alongside the standard waves, Special Eurobarometer surveys explore particular themes in depth, and Flash Eurobarometers, carried out mainly by telephone or online, give quick snapshots on specific questions.

Because the same questions are asked over many years, the surveys allow trends to be tracked, such as trust in the Union or concern about climate change. Critics note, however, that the wording of a question can shape the answers, and that results from small national samples carry larger margins of error.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. About 1,000 people are interviewed in Malta in each standard wave.
B. Malta's national results carry wider margins of error than larger countries'.
C. Flash Eurobarometers usually interview more people per country than standard waves.
D. Since the pandemic, all Eurobarometer interviews have been conducted online.
