## v02-01-c

The European Citizens' Initiative allows citizens to invite the European Commission to propose legislation in an area where the EU has the power to act. An initiative must be organised by at least seven citizens living in seven different Member States, and it must collect at least one million signatures within twelve months. Before collection begins, the Commission checks whether the initiative is admissible. ## v02-02-c

National courts are the ordinary courts of EU law: they apply it in the cases that come before them. When a national court is unsure how a provision of EU law should be interpreted, or whether an act of an EU institution is valid, it may refer a question to the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU). A court whose decisions cannot be challenged under national law, typically a supreme court, is in principle obliged to refer, although the CJEU accepts exceptions, for example where the correct interpretation is so obvious as to leave no reasonable doubt.

The CJEU does not decide the dispute itself: it answers the question, and the national court applies that answer to the facts of the case. Its interpretation also binds other national courts dealing with the same issue. Only the CJEU can declare an EU act invalid; national courts may reject arguments that an act is invalid, but they cannot set it aside on their own authority.

## v02-03-c

The European Court of Auditors, based in Luxembourg, is the EU's independent external auditor. Despite its name, it is not a court of law and has no judicial powers. It checks that EU funds are correctly accounted for, spent in accordance with the rules and achieve value for money.

Suspected fraud is passed to the European Anti-Fraud Office (OLAF), which can investigate, or to the European Public Prosecutor's Office. ## v02-04-c

## v02-06-c

## v02-08-c

Members of the European Parliament are elected every five years by direct universal suffrage. ## v02-24-c

A heat pump does not generate heat by burning fuel. Instead, it uses electricity to move heat from one place to another, for example from the outside air, the ground or water into a building. Because it moves heat rather than creating it, a heat pump can deliver several units of heat for each unit of electricity it consumes.

Air-source heat pumps work even when the outside air is cold, but their efficiency falls as the temperature difference between outside and inside increases. Ground-source systems draw on the more stable temperature below the surface and are therefore more efficient in winter, but they cost more to install. Heat pumps work best in well-insulated buildings with large radiators or underfloor heating, because they deliver heat at lower temperatures than gas boilers. The running cost depends on the price of electricity relative to that of gas, which differs considerably between countries; where electricity is expensive, savings on bills can be small even though the heat pump uses less energy overall.

Cities are often several degrees warmer than the surrounding countryside, especially at night. This 'urban heat island' effect arises because materials such as asphalt, concrete and brick absorb solar heat during the day and release it slowly after sunset, while buildings, air conditioning and traffic produce additional waste heat. Cities also have less vegetation, so less cooling comes from evaporation, and tall buildings can trap heat.

During heatwaves the effect increases health risks, especially for older people, young children and people with chronic illnesses, partly because high night-time temperatures prevent the body from recovering from the heat of the day. Temperatures can vary considerably between neighbourhoods, and poorer districts with fewer trees are often among the hottest. Planting trees, creating parks and green roofs, and using lighter-coloured surfaces that reflect more sunlight can reduce temperatures locally. Air conditioning protects people indoors but releases heat outside, so planners increasingly combine it with passive measures such as shading and ventilation.
