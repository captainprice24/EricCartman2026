# Blind review: verbal-15

For each item, choose the one statement the text supports. Label every other option False (the text contradicts it), Not supported (the text does not say it) or Overstated (stronger than the text). Use only the text.

## v15-01

A fictional airport assigns a night slot only to an operator with a valid noise certificate and either a permanent entitlement or a temporary public-service allocation. A permanent entitlement ceases when the operator transfers the aircraft to a separate legal company. Sharing a trading name does not preserve it. Temporary allocations require a regional request unless the route serves an island without a daytime ferry.

A regional request is valid only after two municipalities approve the same timetable. A council officer can approve on behalf of a municipality when its council has delegated timetable decisions; delegation for ticket subsidies alone is insufficient. Noise certificates attach to an aircraft configuration. Replacing an engine invalidates the certificate until a new test is completed. Tests may be performed locally or at an approved foreign facility; either must use the configuration that will fly. The slot register omits supporting municipal and technical records.

A night slot was validly assigned after the entitled operator transferred the aircraft and its operation to a separate legal company, and the aircraft received a replacement engine. Its route served an inland town. The first municipality's council approved the timetable, while an officer acted for the second. No other entitlement, public-service exception or approval route existed. The public register names no new noise test or council delegation.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The shared trading name preserved the aircraft’s permanent entitlement even though its registered operator had become a separate legal company.
B. The foreign facility that performed the noise test had probably also tested the aircraft before its engine replacement and change of legal owner.
C. A post-replacement noise test and timetable delegation to the second municipal officer were necessary for the valid night allocation.
D. The regional authority requested the temporary allocation after both municipal approvals, on the same calendar day that the second officer signed.

## v15-02

A fictional tissue bank permits an identified sample to leave the bank only for a consent-covered study or under a specific statutory order. General research consent covers laboratory analysis but excludes contact with donors. Recontact counts as part of the study even when an independent contractor conducts it. A statutory order applies only to the disease named in the order, not every condition examined by its recipient.

An amended consent can add recontact only when donors receive the new information and affirmatively agree. Silence is insufficient. Identification keys may be released to the study team only if its named data custodian has current confidentiality accreditation. Accreditation expires on the date printed on it; applying for renewal does not extend validity. A separate accredited custodian can receive the keys instead, but must then handle every donor contact without giving the study team access to identifying details.

The bank validly released identified samples and their keys to a study team investigating condition P and planning donor interviews. Its statutory order concerned condition Q alone. The team's former custodian's accreditation had expired before release; that person had only applied for renewal. Interviews were to be conducted by the team itself. No other consent or accreditation route applied.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The statutory order for condition Q authorised the team’s investigation of condition P because both conditions were examined by its researchers.
B. The former custodian’s renewal application extended accreditation through the sample release even though no new accreditation had been issued.
C. The donors who agreed to the amendment had previously taken part in interviews conducted by the same study team about condition Q.
D. Affirmative recontact consent and a currently accredited replacement custodian were necessary for the team’s receipt of identified samples and keys.

## v15-03

A fictional historical inquiry distinguishes an object's manufacture, dispatch and arrival. An authentic customs stamp dates the stamped object's presence at a border, but a later reproduction of that stamp dates nothing about the copy's journey. A ledger entry quoting a crate number proves dispatch of the crate, without identifying its individual contents or their seals. Individual seal numbers are never reused.

The surviving ledger records a crate dispatched on 4 June, without listing contents. An authenticated border photograph on 9 June shows a parcel with individual seal R inside that crate. A receipt dated 12 June records the first opening of that parcel since it received seal R and lists a notebook inside. The notebook's cover carries a reproduced customs stamp dated 2 June. Examination establishes that the cover and stamp were produced together on paper first available on 6 June. The photograph identifies the same parcel seal as the receipt. The cover was already attached before the notebook entered the parcel.

No notebook could be inserted without opening the parcel. The inquiry confirms the authenticity of the photograph and receipt, the paper analysis and the ledger entry. All dates use the same year and calendar. It has no evidence identifying the person who packed the notebook or recording when the parcel itself was dispatched.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The notebook entered the photographed parcel between 6 and 9 June; the crate’s ledger date does not establish that notebook’s dispatch date.
B. The reproduced customs stamp establishes that the notebook itself crossed the border on 2 June, before dispatch of the crate listed in the ledger.
C. The notebook was inserted at the border by the customs official who appeared beside the parcel in the authenticated 9 June photograph.
D. The cover reproduction was produced on 6 June by the same workshop that supplied replacement seals to the parcel’s dispatch office.

## v15-04

A fictional energy reserve releases fuel when a valid emergency declaration and an eligible operator request coincide. A declaration can come from the national minister or a regional authority with delegated powers. Regional delegation is suspended during an appeal against the delegation itself, until the appeal is decided. An appeal against a particular fuel decision does not suspend general delegation.

A request is eligible through licensed ownership or a valid operating lease. Ownership licensing does not transfer automatically with the fuel facility: the new owner needs its own licence. A lease qualifies only when the lessor has authorised emergency withdrawals and the lessee has provided a current financial guarantee. A bank guarantee expires when its stated term ends. A parent-company guarantee is also accepted, but only from a parent whose audited liquid assets exceed the requested fuel's value. Unaudited management accounts cannot establish that condition.

Fuel was validly released to a new facility owner whose own licence had not been issued. The regional authority declared the emergency while an appeal against its delegation was pending. The national minister also made a declaration. The operator had no current bank guarantee; its earlier one had expired. No request route other than ownership or lease existed, and no exception to the guarantee rules applied.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The appeal against the regional delegation left its powers active because it concerned the delegation rather than a particular fuel decision.
B. Ministerial authority, an authorised operating lease and a parent guarantee supported by sufficient audited liquid assets underpinned the release.
C. The new facility owner inherited the former owner’s licence automatically when it acquired the facility and requested emergency fuel.
D. The parent company’s audited liquid assets were held at the same bank that had issued the operator’s expired financial guarantee.

## v15-05

A fictional journal lifts an embargo for a manuscript only when an eligible editor authorises release and the relevant data are available. Editorial eligibility comes from a current appointment or a special guest mandate. An ordinary appointment ends on resignation; retaining an institutional email address does not continue it. A guest mandate covers only the subject named in the mandate and requires the publisher's written acceptance.

Data availability can be met through public deposition or controlled access. A repository counts as public only when readers can download the actual records without applying for permission; a public landing page is insufficient. Controlled access requires an independent access committee and a binding obligation to decide applications within thirty days. The manuscript's authors cannot constitute that committee, even if they publish their selection criteria. A data-provider promise to consider applications has no binding decision deadline unless an agreement supplies one.

The embargo was validly lifted for an ecology manuscript. Its authorising editor had resigned and had no new ordinary appointment. The records were held only in a permission-only repository with a public landing page. The data provider had issued only a general promise to consider requests. The former editor's email still worked. No route outside those described was available.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The public landing page made the records publicly available even though readers still needed permission to download the actual data.
B. The publisher probably chose the former editor for the guest mandate because that person had previously handled the journal’s ecology manuscripts.
C. An accepted ecology guest mandate and independent controlled access with a binding thirty-day decision duty were required for release.
D. The access committee received more applications after the embargo was lifted than it had received before the journal announced release.

## v15-06

A fictional safety inquiry compares two explanations for a machine failure: an electrical fault or a mechanical obstruction. A diagnostic mark appears after either explanation, so observing that mark alone cannot distinguish them. A melted upstream fuse is expected after an electrical fault only when the protection circuit was connected. Under an obstruction, that fuse can melt only if an unrelated electrical surge also occurred. The inquiry allows both a primary failure and an unrelated surge.

The maintenance log states that the protection circuit was disconnected before the accident. Two witnesses report seeing a flash, but both were repeating the same operator's radio message rather than watching the machine. A nearby monitor recorded a surge during the relevant minute, though its location does not establish that the surge reached this machine. A bench reconstruction produces the diagnostic mark during obstruction without a flash when the protection circuit is disconnected.

The failed machine bears the diagnostic mark and a melted upstream fuse. The inquiry has not established whether the machine received the monitored surge. It treats the log, reconstruction and physical observations as reliable, and regards the two primary explanations as the alternatives under consideration. It has no rule excluding an additional unrelated event.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The two witnesses independently observed a flash at the machine, so their agreement supplies separate evidence against a mechanical obstruction.
B. The bench reconstruction’s obstruction was caused by the same component that obstructed the failed machine during the actual accident.
C. The monitored surge reached the machine before the operator sent the radio message that was later repeated by both witnesses.
D. Neither the repeated flash reports nor the melted fuse resolves which primary explanation accounts for the failed machine’s diagnostic mark.

## v15-07

A fictional scholarship requires residence qualification and an eligible course. Residence qualifies through three continuous years in the district immediately preceding the award or through dependent status under a resident sponsor. An absence exceeding sixty days breaks continuity unless it was an approved placement abroad. Approval must predate departure; later recognition of the placement does not repair continuity. Dependent status ends when the applicant enters full-time employment.

A course qualifies by public accreditation or by an individual equivalence decision. Public accreditation applies to the named campus, not every campus operated by the institution. Equivalence requires an external assessor's report unless the course has an existing decision for the identical campus and curriculum. A curriculum change removes that reuse exception. Assessors must be independent of the applicant and the institution. The scholarship register publishes neither placement approvals nor equivalence reports.

A scholarship was validly awarded to Orin during full-time employment. The course was at an unaccredited branch and had changed curriculum since its older equivalence decision. Orin had lived locally for four years, including one ninety-day foreign placement during the year before award, and no other long absence. The placement was later recognised by the institution. No additional residence or course route applied.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. Advance placement approval and independently assessed equivalence for the altered branch course were necessary for Orin’s scholarship.
B. The institution’s later recognition of the foreign placement restored residence continuity even if no approval existed when Orin left the district.
C. Orin retained dependent status under the resident sponsor throughout full-time employment and therefore needed no qualifying residence period.
D. The external assessor had examined the same institution’s course before its curriculum changed and before the branch campus began teaching it.

## v15-08

A fictional medicines unit releases an unchanged formulation under an existing stability approval. Changing the active ingredient's supplier makes a formulation changed, even if the molecule and trade name remain the same. A changed formulation requires either a recognised supplier bridge or a new stability study. A supplier bridge covers the named manufacturing site; moving production to another site cancels it until the regulator issues a replacement.

A new stability study uses accelerated testing or a full-duration observation. Accelerated testing is unavailable for a formulation containing an ingredient that changes crystal form under heat. Full-duration observation requires calibrated storage chambers throughout the observation period. Replacing a chamber's humidity controller cancels its calibration. Recalibration can be internal or external, but both routes require a humidity reference certified after its latest membrane replacement. A manufacturer's original certificate is insufficient if the membrane has subsequently changed. Public release notices omit chamber and reference records.

A batch was validly released after its supplier changed. No recognised bridge covered the new supplier's manufacturing site; the only recognised bridge named a different site. An ingredient changed crystal form under heat. The reference membrane was replaced before the chamber controller, and observation began after both replacements. No other chamber, study or release route existed.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The unchanged molecule preserved the existing stability approval even though the new supplier manufactured the ingredient at a different site.
B. The testing reference needed fresh certification following its membrane change, a prerequisite for chamber renewal and the required full-length observation.
C. The humidity reference was certified after the chamber controller was replaced, rather than earlier but still after its own membrane replacement.
D. The reference was certified by the same laboratory that recalibrated the chamber before observation of the changed formulation began.

## v15-09

A fictional expedition authenticates a specimen's depth only if its capture cartridge remained uniquely sealed from sampling until laboratory receipt. A cartridge can be opened during transfer without losing authentication only when a trained observer watches continuously and applies a newly logged seal before handling stops. A camera recording can replace that observer only if it shows the cartridge interior as well as the handling area. Recording the outside of a closed transport box is insufficient.

Laboratory receipt requires matching the current individual seal, not merely the expedition's box number. A specimen's species can be identified from a fragment without establishing depth or complete custody. An observer's training authorisation ends when the sampling protocol changes, unless a supplementary assessment under the new protocol is passed. Previous field experience is not a substitute for that assessment. The public species catalogue omits most seal and personnel records.

A specimen's depth was validly authenticated after its cartridge was opened at transfer. The only recording showed the exterior of its transport box. Observer Tavi attended continuously, but Tavi's published training certificate preceded a protocol change before this transfer. No other observer, recording or custody exception applied.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The exterior camera recording replaced the need for an authorised observer because it showed the transport box throughout the cartridge transfer.
B. Tavi’s earlier field experience preserved training authorisation automatically after the sampling protocol changed before the observed transfer took place.
C. Post-change authorisation for Tavi and a new individual seal matched at receipt were necessary for the specimen’s authenticated depth.
D. The laboratory used the same fragment to determine the specimen’s species and to calculate its precise depth below the expedition vessel.

## v15-10

A fictional joint venture can approve a disposal only with both a valid ordinary majority and the infrastructure shareholder's consent. Ordinary voting rights follow registered shares, except that shares pledged without retained voting rights count for the lender. An abstention is not a vote in favour. The infrastructure consent can be given by its board or an officer with a disposal-specific delegation; authority for routine operating expenditure is insufficient.

Board consent requires three unconflicted directors, including an independent director. A director employed by the proposed purchaser is conflicted. An alternate can replace an absent director but not an attending conflicted director. A disposal-specific officer delegation ceases after the infrastructure shareholder merges into another legal company; the successor board must renew it. The venture may publish a decision without publishing its supporting delegation documents.

The disposal was validly approved after such a merger. The ordinary vote had a sufficient registered majority after all pledges were accounted for. No infrastructure consent had been given before this meeting. At the infrastructure meeting, two unconflicted directors and a purchaser-employed director attended, alongside the latter's alternate. Officer Sera gave the consent under a pre-merger delegation. No other board member or consenting person participated.

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

A. The alternate inherited the purchaser-employed director’s seat for this decision even though that director attended the meeting and remained conflicted.
B. Sera’s authority for routine operating expenditure alone was sufficient to provide the infrastructure consent after the shareholder’s legal merger.
C. The ordinary shareholders knew that the successor board had renewed Sera’s disposal-specific delegation before they cast the votes supplying the required registered majority.
D. The successor board had renewed Sera’s disposal mandate, because the attending conflicted director’s alternate could not supply valid board consent.
